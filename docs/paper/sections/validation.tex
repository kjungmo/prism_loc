\section{Validation: Correctness Testing and Synthetic
Evaluation}\label{sec:validation}

\prismloc{} is validated at the level of \emph{correctness}, not field
accuracy, in two complementary ways. First, a deterministic unit- and
integration-test suite pins each estimator component to its specified
behavior (Section~\ref{sec:tests}). Second, a set of closed-loop
synthetic experiments drives the same estimator cores through
end-to-end localization scenarios and reports quantitative behavior
(Section~\ref{sec:synth}). Both run with no middleware, no simulator,
and no recorded bags, because every estimator is a ROS-free library
(Section~\ref{sec:cores}).

\subsection{Correctness Testing}\label{sec:tests}

The test suite comprises 44 GoogleTest cases across four
packages: 25 in the shared \texttt{prism\_loc\_core} (the two MCL
backends and global recovery), 13 in \texttt{prism\_loc\_fusion} (the
ESKF), 4 in the \texttt{prism\_loc} ROS node's adapter layer, and 2 in
\texttt{prism\_loc\_fusion\_ros}.

\textbf{What the cores are pinned to.}
The core tests target the components most likely to regress silently.
The odometry motion model is checked both deterministically
(zero-command and pure-translation cases) and statistically (the mean
of noisy samples approximates the commanded motion). Measurement models
are checked for the monotonicity property they must satisfy to be usable
as a likelihood: the ground-truth pose must out-score a displaced one,
for both the 2D likelihood field and the 3D NDT voxel score. The
particle-filter mechanics---low-variance resampling concentrating on a
heavy particle, and the weighted-mean estimate---are tested directly, as
are the occupancy-grid and NDT-map primitives (world/map round-trips,
out-of-bounds handling, likelihood-field distances, and voxel-mean
recovery). Global recovery is exercised by having branch-and-bound
recover the correct global pose from a single scan---the kidnapped-robot
scenario---and reject an empty scan. Two integration tests close the
loop end to end, driving the full predict--weight--resample cycle until
the estimate converges to ground truth for the \texttt{laser2d} and
\texttt{ndt3d} backends respectively.

\textbf{Fusion and math.}
The ESKF is pinned for both convergence and consistency: a static IMU
leaves the state at rest, constant acceleration reproduces the expected
kinematics, and position and pose updates pull the state toward the
measurement; an integration test tracks a moving target from a
deliberately wrong initial guess. Supporting identities are verified
independently---$SO(3)$ exponential/logarithm round-trips and the
skew/cross-product relation, geodetic ENU round-trips against a datum,
and the \texttt{map}$\to$\texttt{odom} TF composition arithmetic used to
publish the correction transform.

\textbf{Determinism.}
Every stochastic path draws from a single seedable generator exposed as
the ROS parameter \texttt{seed} (default \texttt{42}). The RNG tests
assert that equal seeds reproduce identical sequences and that distinct
seeds differ, so the statistical assertions above are reproducible run
to run rather than flaky.

\textbf{Continuous integration.}
Two CI jobs guard the contract from both sides. A \emph{core} job builds
\texttt{prism\_loc\_core} and \texttt{prism\_loc\_fusion} with plain
CMake and runs \texttt{ctest}, requiring only a compiler and Eigen---no
ROS---which keeps the cores honestly middleware-independent. A separate
\emph{ROS} job builds inside a \texttt{ros:humble} container, resolves
dependencies with \texttt{rosdep}, and runs \texttt{colcon build} and
\texttt{colcon test} across all four packages, so the ROS~2 wrapping is
tested against a real distribution.

\subsection{Synthetic Evaluation}\label{sec:synth}

Beyond per-component assertions, three closed-loop experiments drive
the \emph{actual} estimator cores---compiled directly from
\texttt{prism\_loc\_core} and \texttt{prism\_loc\_fusion} sources with
\texttt{g++} and Eigen, no ROS and no CMake---through end-to-end
localization scenarios. All data are synthetic: worlds, scans, and
inertial streams are generated by the experiment drivers themselves,
with all randomness derived from fixed compile-time seeds, so every
number and figure below is exactly reproducible from the drivers and
CSV logs shipped in the repository (\texttt{experiments/}). We stress
that this is a \emph{correctness and behavior demonstration on
synthetic data}, not a field-accuracy benchmark: no real robot or
sensor is involved, and the sensor-noise models match the assumptions
of the filters by construction. No external baseline is run: neither a
tracker such as \texttt{nav2\_amcl}, Beluga~\cite{puga2024beluga},
MH-AMCL~\cite{garcia2023mhamcl}, or
\texttt{hdl\_localization}~\cite{koide2019portable}, nor a global
localizer such as \texttt{hdl\_global\_localization}~\cite{koide2021hdlgl}
or CBGL~\cite{filotheou2024cbgl}. Every result below concerns
\prismloc{}'s own cores, and a comparative study on real and public data
is left to future work (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\textbf{MCL pose tracking and KLD adaptation.}
The first experiment exercises the \texttt{laser2d} pipeline exactly as
\texttt{localization\_node.cpp} wires it. A $12\,\mathrm{m} \times
10\,\mathrm{m}$ occupancy grid ($0.1$\,m/cell, the integration test's
room-border convention plus two interior pillars that break pose
symmetry) hosts a simulated robot driven around a closed oval loop with
semi-axes $3.6\,\mathrm{m}$ and $2.6\,\mathrm{m}$
($7.2\,\mathrm{m} \times 5.2\,\mathrm{m}$ extent) for 65\,s at 10\,Hz
(650 steps). At each step a 180-beam scan is ray-cast at the true pose,
and a drifting odometry frame is synthesized by integrating
noise-corrupted motion increments under the \texttt{laser2d.yaml}
odometry model ($\alpha_{1..4}=0.2$). The real
\texttt{ParticleFilter} with the \texttt{Laser2DLikelihoodField} model
and KLD-adaptive resampling---every parameter taken from
\texttt{laser2d.yaml}---is seeded with a broad Gaussian prior
($\sigma = 1$\,m, $0.4$\,rad) centered at the true start pose and
updated in the node's
predict--correct--resample order, gated by
$\texttt{update\_min\_d} = 0.2$\,m and
$\texttt{update\_min\_a} = 0.2$\,rad. Three fixed seeds (101, 202, 303)
are run. Fig.~\ref{fig:mcl} shows the resulting position and yaw error:
after convergence ($t \geq 10$\,s, across all three seeds) the mean
position error is 0.105\,m (max 0.29\,m) and the mean yaw error is
0.032\,rad $=$ 1.8$^\circ$ (max 0.18\,rad). Fig.~\ref{fig:kld} shows
the KLD-adaptive particle count over the same runs: the set starts at
the 2000-particle ceiling under the broad prior, collapses to the
500-particle floor after the first post-convergence update, and holds
there for the rest of the loop---a $4\times$ reduction in per-update
likelihood evaluations once the posterior is concentrated.

\textbf{Global relocalization by branch-and-bound.}
The second experiment evaluates the BBS matcher
(Section~\ref{sec:global}) as a kidnapped-robot relocalizer. On the
asymmetric $6\,\mathrm{m} \times 6\,\mathrm{m}$ room from the BBS unit
test (two interior wall stubs break the four-fold symmetry, so a single
360$^\circ$ scan pins the global pose uniquely), $N=100$ true poses are
drawn uniformly over free space with uniform yaw (fixed seed 20240607),
and one 180-beam scan is ray-cast per pose. To span an easy-to-hard
range, each scan is corrupted with synthetic clutter: a per-query
clutter fraction drawn uniformly in $[0, 0.8]$ replaces each beam, with
that probability, by a uniform-random spurious range---modeling people,
glass, and objects absent from the static map. The real
\texttt{BranchAndBoundMatcher} then searches the whole map with the
\texttt{laser2d.yaml} \texttt{bbs\_*} defaults. Defining success as
position error $< 0.5$\,m and yaw error $< 10^\circ$, 97 of 100 queries
succeed (97.0\%); over all 100 queries the recovered pose has median
position error 0.037\,m (95th percentile 0.072\,m) and median yaw error
0.48$^\circ$ (95th percentile 1.74$^\circ$). Fig.~\ref{fig:bbs} plots
position error against the BBS score fraction (raw score over used
beams): the acceptance threshold
$\texttt{bbs\_min\_score\_fraction}=0.40$ yields \emph{zero false
accepts}---all 3 failures score in $0.21$--$0.27$ with $>3$\,m and
roughly $90^\circ$--$180^\circ$ error, while 78 matches are accepted
(all correct) and 19 correct fixes are conservatively rejected. The
median score fraction is 0.632; successes span $0.22$--$0.97$ while all
failures fall below $0.28$. Every query scoring at or above the $0.40$
acceptance threshold is correct, so the threshold separates accepted
matches from failures with a wide margin, even though three low-scoring
successes overlap the failure score band. Median full-map query latency is 37.65\,ms (min 8.77\,ms, 95th
percentile 106.06\,ms; single-threaded, machine-dependent wall-clock;
all accuracy numbers are deterministic across runs).

\textbf{ESKF fusion under aiding dropout.}
The third experiment drives the actual \texttt{prism\_loc\_fusion}
\texttt{Eskf} over a synthetic 60\,s 3-D trajectory: a horizontal
figure-eight ($x = 8\sin\omega t$, $y = 4\sin 2\omega t$,
$\omega = 2\pi/30$) with a gentle $\pm 1.5$\,m altitude oscillation.
The ideal body-frame specific force and angular rate computed from this
analytic trajectory are corrupted with exactly the white-noise
densities and constant biases the ESKF process model assumes
(\texttt{fusion3d.yaml} defaults), plus fixed true biases the filter
must estimate from zero; all noise derives from a single fixed seed
(20240517). The IMU runs at 200\,Hz, a simulated NDT source delivers
6-DoF pose updates at 10\,Hz, and a simulated GNSS source delivers
position updates at 1\,Hz, with injected measurement noise matched to
the filter's $R$ matrices. A 10\,s window ($t \in [25, 35)$\,s)
withholds both aiding streams to expose IMU-only dead reckoning and
recovery. Fig.~\ref{fig:eskf} shows the position-error norm against the
filter's own $3\sigma$ position bound: windowed position RMSE is
0.016\,m before, 0.125\,m during, and 0.009\,m after the dropout, with
a peak error of 0.256\,m at $t = 34.5$\,s (end of the IMU-only
blackout) recovering to 0.015\,m within one aiding cycle by
$t = 35$\,s. The $3\sigma$ bound inflates from roughly 0.03\,m under
full aiding to a peak of 0.663\,m at $t = 34.5$\,s and stays above the
true error throughout this run. A single run does not test whether the
reported covariance is statistically consistent, so we make no
consistency claim for it (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}).

\textbf{Scope.}
This validation establishes that the estimators are individually
correct, that all three sit behind one working ROS~2 contract, and that
the assembled pipelines behave as intended in closed loop on synthetic
data; it is deliberately not an accuracy benchmark. The planned
quantitative field evaluation is discussed with the other roadmap items
in Section~\ref{sec:limitations}.
