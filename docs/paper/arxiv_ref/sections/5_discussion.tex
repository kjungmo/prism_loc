\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:disc}

\noindent\textbf{What the Factoring Buys.} Because the MCL core is written against one \code{MeasurementModel} interface, the 2D and 3D backends share the motion model, weighting, KLD resampling, estimate, and every test of those components; adding a backend means adding one likelihood function. Because the cores are middleware-free, every equation of \Cref{sec:method} is exercised by deterministic tests and by the closed-loop drivers of \Cref{sec:exp} without a ROS installation, which is also what makes the evaluation reproducible bit for bit from the repository.

\noindent\textbf{Cost Analysis.} The per-update cost of the MCL backends is linear in the particle count and in the number of scored beams or points (\Cref{eq:laser,eq:ndt}); KLD adaptation keeps the former at the 500-particle floor once the posterior is concentrated (\Cref{fig:mcl}). The branch-and-bound matcher's cost is data-dependent through pruning: on the synthetic room a full-map query took a median of 37.65\,ms (min 8.77\,ms, 95th percentile 106.07\,ms) on the machine that produced the committed data, and it runs only when relocalization is requested, not every scan. Because the map-sized window grows with map area, so does the cost: on the 80\,m map of \Cref{tab:bbs_large} the median query takes 1526\,ms, which is acceptable for an on-demand recovery but not for per-scan use; \code{bbs\_max\_linear\_window} bounds it at the price of global coverage. The ESKF step is a fixed $15\times15$ covariance propagation per IMU sample.

\noindent\textbf{Limitations.} \sname{} is an architecture paper, and its scope is deliberately bounded.
(i)~\emph{Planar state in the MCL backends.} Both particle-filter backends estimate $(x,y,\theta)$; \code{ndt3d} evaluates a full 3D Gaussian-voxel map from that planar pose at a fixed base height, which suits wheeled robots on locally flat floors but does not track roll, pitch, or vertical motion. Full $SE(3)$ state exists only in \code{fusion3d}.
(ii)~\emph{2D-only relocalization with an imperfect acceptance test.} \Cref{alg:bbs} operates on a 2D occupancy pyramid; \code{ndt3d} and \code{fusion3d} rely on an operator-supplied or GNSS-derived initial pose and local tracking. The search window is sized from the map and restricted to non-occupied cells, but the only guard against a wrong optimum is the score fraction of \Cref{eq:bbsaccept}, and on maps with repeated structure it is not enough: at the default $\rho$ the matcher accepted a wrong pose on 6 of 120 (40\,m) and 15 of 120 (80\,m) synthetic queries, and no single threshold removes these on the two maps together without rejecting more than half of the correct fixes. An accepted match replaces the particle set at once, and relocalization runs only at startup or on a service call; no loss detector triggers it. Verifying the top candidates, e.g., by running MCL on several hypotheses before committing, would address this and is not implemented. Beyond the compute cap (100\,m per axis by default) the search is no longer global, which the node reports at startup.
(iii)~\emph{No time-offset or extrinsic estimation.} The ESKF treats the LiDAR/GNSS-to-IMU time offset and the LiDAR--IMU extrinsic as known inputs.
(iv)~\emph{Loosely-coupled LiDAR update.} In \code{fusion3d}, PCL NDT produces a 6-DoF pose measurement that the ESKF fuses as a loosely-coupled update, which keeps the filter core independent of the registration engine but forgoes the accuracy of tightly-coupled formulations such as FAST-LIO \citep{xu2021fastlio}. The filter is also not NEES-consistent on our synthetic scenario (\Cref{tab:nees}): it is overconfident, most of all in attitude and, while aiding is absent, in position and velocity, whereas its bias blocks are consistent; the optional reset Jacobian of \citet{sola2017quaternion} does not change this. Consumers should treat the published orientation covariance, and the position covariance during aiding outages, with caution until the cause is found.
(v)~\emph{Single static map.} All backends assume one prior map of a static world, loaded once, with no online map update or change detection.
(vi)~\emph{No field-accuracy evaluation.} The evaluation of \Cref{sec:exp} is deterministic unit and integration testing plus a seeded synthetic study whose worlds and sensor-noise models match the filters' assumptions by construction, and it runs no external baseline. It establishes that each core behaves as specified in closed loop, not how accurate \sname{} is on a physical robot. We have not measured trajectory error on physical or public datasets; the planned evaluation will report absolute and relative trajectory error (ATE/RPE) following the TUM RGB-D conventions \citep{sturm2012benchmark} on the per-backend \code{map}$\to$\code{odom} estimates. Until then we make no accuracy claim.

\noindent\textbf{Future Work.} Extending the MCL core to a 6-DoF particle state or coupling it to an external attitude source, hypothesis verification for the 2D relocalizer and a 3D branch-and-bound or place-recognition front end for the 3D backends, a diagnosis of the ESKF's attitude overconfidence, joint time-offset and extrinsic estimation in the ESKF, and the public-dataset evaluation above are the next steps on the roadmap.
