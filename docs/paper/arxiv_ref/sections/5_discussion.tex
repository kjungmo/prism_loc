\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:disc}

\noindent\textbf{What the Factoring Buys.} Because the MCL core is written against one \code{MeasurementModel} interface, the 2D and 3D backends share the motion model, weighting, KLD resampling, estimate, and every test of those components; adding a backend means adding one likelihood function. This is the same benefit that Beluga's shared core provides \citep{puga2024beluga}, and we report it as a property of the artifact rather than as a contribution. Because the cores are middleware-free, every equation of \Cref{sec:method} is exercised by deterministic tests and by the closed-loop drivers of \Cref{sec:exp} without a ROS installation, which is also what makes the synthetic evaluation reproducible bit for bit from the repository.

\noindent\textbf{Cost Analysis.} The per-update cost of the MCL backends is linear in the particle count and in the number of scored beams or points (\Cref{eq:laser,eq:ndt}); in the tracking run the particle count stays at the 500-particle floor after convergence (\Cref{fig:mcl}). The branch-and-bound matcher's cost is data-dependent through pruning: on the synthetic room a full-map query took a median of 37.65\,ms (min 8.77\,ms, 95th percentile 106.07\,ms) on the machine that produced the committed data, and it runs only when relocalization is requested, not every scan. Because the map-sized window grows with map area, so does the cost: on the 80\,m map of \Cref{tab:bbs_large} the median query takes 1526\,ms, which is acceptable for an on-demand recovery but not for per-scan use; \code{bbs\_max\_linear\_window} bounds it at the price of global coverage. Keeping the top eight modes weakens the pruning, and the local re-matches of the later scans are cheap, so in \Cref{tab:bbs_verify} a verified decision costs roughly 1.3 times the single-scan query on the 80\,m map, plus the time to drive eight more scans. The ESKF step is a fixed $15\times15$ covariance propagation per IMU sample.

\noindent\textbf{Limitations.} \sname{} is a systems paper that integrates established components, and its scope is deliberately bounded.
(i)~\emph{Planar state in the MCL backends.} Both particle-filter backends estimate $(x,y,\theta)$; \code{ndt3d} evaluates a full 3D Gaussian-voxel map from that planar pose at a fixed base height, which suits wheeled robots on locally flat floors but does not track roll, pitch, or vertical motion. On NCLT, a constant base height fails on an 18\,m height change (\Cref{tab:elev}); the optional ground grid follows the ground height under each particle, but the state stays planar. Full $SE(3)$ state exists only in \code{fusion3d}; existing particle-filter packages already estimate a full 3D pose against NDT or point-cloud maps \citep{puga2024beluga,ekumen2022belugarepo,watanabe2016mcl3dl}.
(ii)~\emph{2D-only relocalization with a heuristic verification.} \Cref{alg:bbs} operates on a 2D occupancy pyramid; \code{ndt3d} and \code{fusion3d} rely on an operator-supplied or GNSS-derived initial pose and local tracking. The search window is sized from the map and restricted to non-occupied cells. On maps with repeated structure the score fraction of \Cref{eq:bbsaccept} alone does not separate look-alike places: a single scan accepted a wrong pose on 6 of 120 (40\,m) and 15 of 120 (80\,m) synthetic queries, and no single threshold removes these on the two maps together without rejecting more than half of the correct fixes. Multi-scan verification (\Cref{sec:verify}) reduces them to 0 and 1, but it is a heuristic: its evidence score (named a posterior in the code) treats correlated scans as independent evidence behind a tuned temperature and is not a calibrated probability, its parameters were tuned on one synthetic generator (on disjoint seeds), the one residual false accept held 0.969 of the evidence score, it can only choose among the $K$ modes of the first scan, it needs the robot to move, and the top-8 search is slower than the single-best one. It was evaluated on synthetic worlds with simulated odometry and on 50 queries of one public dataset (\Cref{tab:statareloc}), where the odometry is synthesized. Relocalization runs only at startup or on a service call; unlike reliability-aware MCL \citep{akai2023reliable}, no loss detector triggers it. Beyond the compute cap (100\,m per axis by default) the search is no longer global, which the node reports at startup.
(iii)~\emph{No time-offset or extrinsic estimation.} The ESKF treats the LiDAR/GNSS-to-IMU time offset and the LiDAR--IMU extrinsic as known inputs.
(iv)~\emph{Loosely-coupled LiDAR update and an inconsistent covariance.} In \code{fusion3d}, PCL NDT produces a 6-DoF pose measurement that the ESKF fuses as a loosely-coupled update, which keeps the filter core independent of the registration engine but forgoes the accuracy of tightly-coupled formulations such as FAST-LIO \citep{xu2021fastlio}. The filter is also not NEES-consistent on our synthetic scenario (\Cref{tab:nees}): it is overconfident, most of all in attitude and, while aiding is absent, in position and velocity, whereas its bias blocks are consistent; the optional reset Jacobian of \citet{sola2017quaternion} does not change this. We therefore regard \code{fusion3d} as experimental: consumers should treat the published orientation covariance, and the position covariance during aiding outages, with caution until the cause is found.
(v)~\emph{Single static map.} All backends assume one prior map of a static world, loaded once, with no online map update or change detection.
(vi)~\emph{Scope of the public-dataset evaluation.} The synthetic study of \Cref{sec:exp} uses worlds and sensor-noise models that match the filters' assumptions by construction, and every comparison in it is internal. The public evaluation of \Cref{sec:public} is narrow: it covers starts from a wrong initial pose on two datasets, with 12 runs per setting and three seeds, and it is not a benchmark of trajectory accuracy. We list its limits.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{AMCL.} AMCL ran with Nav2 defaults and the settings that match \code{laser2d.yaml} only; other parameter sets were not tested. AMCL cannot be seeded, so its three seeds differ only in the initial offset (and on Stata in the odometry noise), and its runs are not independent filter realisations. It fails even at zero offset on the sparse NCLT projection (6 of 12 runs converge), probably because of its sensor model on sparse scans, which we did not confirm. We therefore use no AMCL baseline on NCLT.
  \item \emph{Height band.} The 2D height band of the NCLT projection was chosen after seeing the L0 results of the same segments. A band fixed in advance gives the same counts at L0, L1, L2, L4, L5, and L6, 8 instead of 9 of 12 at L3, and 9 instead of 12 of 12 at level U.
  \item \emph{Synthesized odometry, optimistic map.} The Stata scan files carry no odometry, so we synthesize it from ground-truth increments plus fixed errors, and the Stata map is built from ground-truth poses. Another odometry model or a SLAM map would change the numbers.
  \item \emph{What ``converged'' counts.} A run counts as converged if the error stays under 1\,m for 10\,s within the first 120\,s. Later losses of the pose show only in the RMSE after convergence. On the 1403\,s Stata query \code{laser2d} loses the pose for about 500\,s in two of three seeds.
  \item \emph{Old map.} With the three-month-old Stata map, tracking from the true start pose is lost in 5 of 6 runs, and the verifier gives 30 correct, 8 wrong, and 12 ambiguous results on 50 queries. We did not confirm that a change of the environment is the cause.
  \item \emph{Setting chosen on the test sessions.} The \code{ndt3d} setting (ground grid, \code{ndt\_effective\_points}\,=\,5, \code{ndt\_min\_sigma}\,=\,0.10\,m) is the best of six tried on the NCLT tracking runs, which use the same sessions as the evaluation, so its numbers are optimistic.
  \item \emph{Fairness.} The particle filter is given the initial spread and the tracker cannot use one. The tracker was run with one registration setting, and the tracker is the more accurate method once converged.
  \item \emph{\code{fusion3d}.} \code{fusion3d} has no public evaluation: a preliminary run on public data was not validated (an IMU axis issue), and we report no numbers from it.
  \item \emph{No other systems.} We did not run Beluga, MH-AMCL, \code{hdl\_global\_localization}, CBGL, or the recovery tooling of \code{lidar\_localization\_ros2} \citep{sasaki2026globalloc}, nor the methods of \citet{zhang2026krp} and \citet{gao2019global}. The false-accept reduction of verification is shown on synthetic data and on 50 Stata queries, not on a physical robot.
\end{itemize}
The ESKF study uses simulated NDT pose measurements, so the PCL NDT registration stage of \code{fusion3d} was not exercised, and no experiment runs the three backends under one common Nav2 configuration, so the shared node contract was not shown that way.
The public evaluation therefore shows how the backends behave on two datasets under the stated conditions. It does not establish accuracy on a physical robot or competitiveness with the systems above. Trajectory error on the per-backend \code{map}$\to$\code{odom} estimates following the TUM RGB-D conventions \citep{sturm2012benchmark} is not measured.

\noindent\textbf{Future Work.} Extending the MCL core to a 6-DoF particle state or coupling it to an external attitude source, re-admitting new candidates during verification and a principled (correlation-aware) evidence model for the 2D relocalizer, a loss detector that triggers it, a 3D branch-and-bound front end such as 3D-BBS \citep{aoki2024bbs3d} or a place-recognition front end for the 3D backends, a diagnosis of the ESKF's attitude overconfidence, joint time-offset and extrinsic estimation in the ESKF, a public evaluation of \code{fusion3d}, other \code{nav2\_amcl} parameter sets, further external systems, and trajectory-error measurements on more public sequences are the next steps on the roadmap.
