\section{Discussion and Limitations}\label{sec:disc}

\noindent\textbf{What the Factoring Buys.} Because the MCL core is written against one \code{MeasurementModel} interface, the 2D and 3D backends share the motion model, weighting, KLD resampling, estimate, and every test of those components; adding a backend means adding one likelihood function. This is the same benefit that Beluga's shared core provides \citep{puga2024beluga}, and we report it as a property of the artifact rather than as a contribution. Because the cores are middleware-free, every equation of \Cref{sec:method} is exercised by deterministic tests and by the closed-loop drivers of \Cref{sec:exp} without a ROS installation, which is also what makes the evaluation reproducible bit for bit from the repository.

\noindent\textbf{Cost Analysis.} The per-update cost of the MCL backends is linear in the particle count and in the number of scored beams or points (\Cref{eq:laser,eq:ndt}); KLD adaptation keeps the former at the 500-particle floor once the posterior is concentrated (\Cref{fig:mcl}). The branch-and-bound matcher's cost is data-dependent through pruning: on the synthetic room a full-map query took a median of 37.65\,ms (min 8.77\,ms, 95th percentile 106.07\,ms) on the machine that produced the committed data, and it runs only when relocalization is requested, not every scan. Because the map-sized window grows with map area, so does the cost: on the 80\,m map of \Cref{tab:bbs_large} the median query takes 1526\,ms, which is acceptable for an on-demand recovery but not for per-scan use; \code{bbs\_max\_linear\_window} bounds it at the price of global coverage. Keeping the top eight modes weakens the pruning, and the local re-matches of the later scans are cheap, so in \Cref{tab:bbs_verify} a verified decision costs roughly 1.3 times the single-scan query on the 80\,m map, plus the time to drive eight more scans. The ESKF step is a fixed $15\times15$ covariance propagation per IMU sample.

\noindent\textbf{Limitations.} \sname{} is a systems paper that integrates established components, and its scope is deliberately bounded.
(i)~\emph{Planar state in the MCL backends.} Both particle-filter backends estimate $(x,y,\theta)$; \code{ndt3d} evaluates a full 3D Gaussian-voxel map from that planar pose at a fixed base height, which suits wheeled robots on locally flat floors but does not track roll, pitch, or vertical motion. Full $SE(3)$ state exists only in \code{fusion3d}; existing particle-filter packages already estimate a full 3D pose against NDT or point-cloud maps \citep{puga2024beluga,ekumen2022belugarepo,watanabe2016mcl3dl}.
(ii)~\emph{2D-only relocalization with a heuristic verification.} \Cref{alg:bbs} operates on a 2D occupancy pyramid; \code{ndt3d} and \code{fusion3d} rely on an operator-supplied or GNSS-derived initial pose and local tracking. The search window is sized from the map and restricted to non-occupied cells. On maps with repeated structure the score fraction of \Cref{eq:bbsaccept} alone does not separate look-alike places: a single scan accepted a wrong pose on 6 of 120 (40\,m) and 15 of 120 (80\,m) synthetic queries, and no single threshold removes these on the two maps together without rejecting more than half of the correct fixes. Multi-scan verification (\Cref{sec:verify}) reduces them to 0 and 1, but it is a heuristic: its evidence score (named a posterior in the code) treats correlated scans as independent evidence behind a tuned temperature and is not a calibrated probability, its parameters were tuned on one synthetic generator (on disjoint seeds), the one residual false accept held 0.969 of the evidence score, it can only choose among the $K$ modes of the first scan, it needs the robot to move, and the top-8 search is slower than the single-best one. It was evaluated only on synthetic worlds with simulated odometry. Relocalization runs only at startup or on a service call; unlike reliability-aware MCL \citep{akai2023reliable}, no loss detector triggers it. Beyond the compute cap (100\,m per axis by default) the search is no longer global, which the node reports at startup.
(iii)~\emph{No time-offset or extrinsic estimation.} The ESKF treats the LiDAR/GNSS-to-IMU time offset and the LiDAR--IMU extrinsic as known inputs.
(iv)~\emph{Loosely-coupled LiDAR update and an inconsistent covariance.} In \code{fusion3d}, PCL NDT produces a 6-DoF pose measurement that the ESKF fuses as a loosely-coupled update, which keeps the filter core independent of the registration engine but forgoes the accuracy of tightly-coupled formulations such as FAST-LIO \citep{xu2021fastlio}. The filter is also not NEES-consistent on our synthetic scenario (\Cref{tab:nees}): it is overconfident, most of all in attitude and, while aiding is absent, in position and velocity, whereas its bias blocks are consistent; the optional reset Jacobian of \citet{sola2017quaternion} does not change this. We therefore regard \code{fusion3d} as experimental: consumers should treat the published orientation covariance, and the position covariance during aiding outages, with caution until the cause is found.
(v)~\emph{Single static map.} All backends assume one prior map of a static world, loaded once, with no online map update or change detection.
(vi)~\emph{No external baseline and no real data.} The evaluation of \Cref{sec:exp} is deterministic unit and integration testing plus a seeded synthetic study whose worlds and sensor-noise models match the filters' assumptions by construction. Every comparison in it is internal, between configurations of \sname{}: we have not compared the trackers with \code{nav2\_amcl}, Beluga \citep{puga2024beluga}, or MH-AMCL \citep{garcia2023mhamcl}, the relocalizer with \code{hdl\_global\_localization} \citep{koide2021hdlgl}, the recovery tooling of \code{lidar\_localization\_ros2} \citep{sasaki2026globalloc}, CBGL \citep{filotheou2024cbgl}, or the method of \citet{zhang2026krp}, or the verifier with the multiple-hypothesis tracker of \citet{gao2019global}. The study therefore establishes that each core behaves as specified in closed loop; it does not establish accuracy on a physical robot, competitiveness with these systems, or that the false-accept reduction transfers to real maps and real odometry. We have not measured trajectory error on physical or public datasets. The planned evaluation runs the three backends and at least \code{nav2\_amcl} and one external global-localization method on public LiDAR sequences, including one with repeated structure, and reports absolute and relative trajectory error (ATE/RPE) following the TUM RGB-D conventions \citep{sturm2012benchmark} on the per-backend \code{map}$\to$\code{odom} estimates, together with relocalization success and false-accept rates. Until then we make no accuracy claim.

\noindent\textbf{Future Work.} Extending the MCL core to a 6-DoF particle state or coupling it to an external attitude source, re-admitting new candidates during verification and a principled (correlation-aware) evidence model for the 2D relocalizer, a loss detector that triggers it, a 3D branch-and-bound front end such as 3D-BBS \citep{aoki2024bbs3d} or a place-recognition front end for the 3D backends, a diagnosis of the ESKF's attitude overconfidence, joint time-offset and extrinsic estimation in the ESKF, and the external-baseline evaluation above are the next steps on the roadmap.
