\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\input{figures/overview}

Localizing a mobile robot against a prior map is, on paper, a solved problem, but in practice it is solved in fragments. A team fielding a 2D LiDAR reaches for \code{nav2\_amcl}, a particle filter that tracks a planar pose against an occupancy grid and nothing else. A team with a 3D LiDAR reaches instead for an \code{hdl\_localization}-style tracker built around 3D NDT registration \citep{koide2019portable}. A team that must fuse inertial and satellite measurements reaches for a \code{robot\_localization}-style node \citep{moore2014generalized}, which composes an EKF over odometry, IMU, and GNSS but treats the map-relative LiDAR measurement as someone else's problem. Each of these tools carries its own topic interface, parameter vocabulary, initialization ritual, and recovery story, so a practitioner who moves from a 2D platform to a 3D one, or who adds a GNSS receiver, re-integrates from scratch.

The gap is recognized by the maintainers of the ROS~2 navigation ecosystem. Their survey \citep{macenski2023survey} catalogs the shortcomings of AMCL and sketches, as unbuilt future work, a modular framework of swappable 2D and 3D localization components behind a common interface. The cost of the gap is not merely inconvenience: the Nav2 marathon deployment \citep{macenski2020marathon} attributes a leading share of its autonomy-recovery triggers to loss of localization confidence, so how a stack initializes, degrades, and recovers is a first-class deployment concern rather than a detail. Classical MCL recovers from such failures through stochastic mechanisms, random particle injection or dual sampling \citep{fox1999efficient,thrun2001robust}, which are hard to reproduce in a test.

To overcome this fragmentation, we introduce \emph{\sname}, an engineering response that packages three map-based LiDAR localization backends behind one ROS~2 node contract (\Cref{fig:overview}): \code{laser2d}, a 2D likelihood-field Monte Carlo localizer against an occupancy grid; \code{ndt3d}, a 3D NDT-MCL backend that reuses the same particle filter with a voxel-Gaussian measurement model over a prior point cloud, sampling the planar $(x,y,\theta)$ pose; and \code{fusion3d}, a 15-state error-state Kalman filter (ESKF) that fuses 3D LiDAR, IMU, and RTK-GNSS with IMU-bias estimation. All three present the identical interface, a \code{map}$\to$\code{odom} transform, a pose with $6\times6$ covariance, and \code{/initialpose} seeding, so choosing between a 2D scan, a 3D cloud, and an inertial/GNSS-aided configuration is a launch-time decision rather than a change of stack. We claim no new estimator theory: likelihood-field MCL, NDT-MCL, ESKF fusion, and branch-and-bound scan matching are all established. The contribution is the factoring, and it is delivered as usable open-source software:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{One Pluggable Contract}: three estimator families (2D likelihood-field MCL, 3D NDT-MCL, and LiDAR+IMU+RTK-GNSS ESKF fusion) present one ROS~2 \code{map}$\to$\code{odom} contract, so the sensor suite selects a backend rather than a separate stack; the release adds fail-fast configuration checks, a startup watchdog that names the input that has gone silent, and a single-source parameter reference.
  \item \emph{Middleware-Free, Deterministic Cores}: all filter mathematics sit behind an Eigen-only boundary with no \code{rclcpp}, \code{tf2}, or PCL, and all sampling routes through one seeded generator, so every algorithmic claim is exercised by 44 deterministic unit and integration tests and by closed-loop synthetic experiments that compile the cores directly.
  \item \emph{Deterministic Global Relocalization}: for \code{laser2d}, branch-and-bound correlative scan matching over a max-pooled likelihood pyramid solves the kidnapped-robot problem from one scan and re-seeds the MCL posterior, replacing stochastic random-injection recovery with a repeatable, globally optimal search over the discretized pose space.
\end{itemize}

We validate \sname{} at the level of correctness and closed-loop behavior, not field accuracy. On seeded synthetic worlds generated to match the filters' assumptions, the \code{laser2d} tracker holds a steady-state mean position error of 0.105\,m while KLD-adaptive resampling shrinks the particle set from the 2000-particle ceiling to the 500-particle floor; branch-and-bound relocalization recovers 97 of 100 kidnapped-robot queries under up to 80\% beam clutter with zero false accepts at its default acceptance threshold; and the ESKF limits a 10\,s loss of all aiding to 0.125\,m position RMSE while its own $3\sigma$ bound stays above the true error throughout. These numbers demonstrate that the assembled pipelines behave as intended; they are not a benchmark, and quantitative evaluation on public datasets remains future work (\Cref{sec:disc}).
