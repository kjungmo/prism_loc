\section{Experiments}\label{sec:exp}

We validate \sname{} at two levels: \emph{correctness and closed-loop behavior} on synthetic data, and \emph{recovery from a wrong initial pose} on two public datasets. We make no field-accuracy claim. \Cref{sec:setup} describes the test suite and the six synthetic closed-loop experiments, \Cref{sec:main} reports their results, and \Cref{sec:ablation} analyzes the sensitivity to individual mechanisms using the same committed runs. All data in these three subsections are synthetic. \Cref{sec:public} then reports the public-dataset evaluation (NCLT and MIT Stata Center), with a 6-DoF NDT tracker and \code{nav2\_amcl} as comparison methods.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}\label{sec:setup}

\noindent\textbf{Correctness Tests.} The suite comprises 63 GoogleTest cases across the four packages: 42 in \code{prism\_loc\_core} (both MCL backends and global recovery), 15 in \code{prism\_loc\_fusion} (the ESKF), 4 in the \code{prism\_loc} adapter layer, and 2 in \code{prism\_loc\_fusion\_ros}. The motion model is checked deterministically (zero-command and pure-translation cases) and statistically (the mean of noisy samples approximates the commanded motion); both measurement models are checked for the monotonicity a likelihood must satisfy (the true pose out-scores a displaced one); resampling, the weighted-mean estimate, the grid and NDT-map primitives, and branch-and-bound recovery of a single-scan kidnapped pose (with rejection of an empty scan) are tested directly, the latter also on a 40\,m$\times$30\,m map with the pose 17\,m from the center, together with the symmetric window, the compute cap, the rejection of occupied and off-map candidates, and bit-exact agreement with exhaustive search (\Cref{cor:bbs}) with three negative controls that perturb the bound; the top-$K$ search and the verifier of \Cref{sec:verify} are tested on twin rooms that are identical within sensor range: the first mode equals the single-best match, both twins are returned with equal score, a scan that fools the single-scan rule (an unmapped box beside the robot matches a mapped box in the other twin) is resolved once driving brings a distinguishing pillar into range, a place that nothing in range distinguishes is reported ambiguous rather than committed, an unambiguous place is accepted at the true pose, and $K=M=1$ reproduces the single-scan result; two integration tests close the \code{laser2d} and \code{ndt3d} loops until convergence to ground truth. The ESKF is pinned for rest under a static IMU, constant-acceleration kinematics, measurement pull-in, and tracking of a moving target from a wrong initial guess; $SO(3)$ exponential/logarithm round trips, geodetic ENU round trips, and the \Cref{eq:mapodom} composition are verified separately, and RNG tests assert that equal seeds reproduce identical sequences; two tests pin the optional ESKF reset Jacobian. Two CI jobs guard the boundary from both sides: a \emph{core} job builds the two cores with plain CMake and runs \code{ctest} with only a compiler and Eigen, and a \emph{ROS} job runs \code{colcon build} and \code{colcon test} for all four packages in a \code{ros:humble} container.

\noindent\textbf{Synthetic Closed-Loop Protocol.} Six drivers under \code{experiments/} compile the \emph{actual} core sources directly with \code{g++} and Eigen (no ROS, no CMake) and generate their own worlds, scans, and inertial streams, with all randomness derived from fixed compile-time seeds. The sensor-noise models match the filters' assumptions by construction, so the experiments demonstrate behavior rather than benchmark accuracy.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{MCL tracking.} A $12\,\mathrm{m}\times10\,\mathrm{m}$ grid at 0.1\,m/cell (room border plus two interior pillars) hosts a robot driven around a closed oval loop with semi-axes 3.6\,m and 2.6\,m for 65\,s at 10\,Hz (650 steps). Each step ray-casts a 180-beam scan at the true pose and synthesizes drifting odometry from noise-corrupted increments under the \code{laser2d.yaml} model ($\alpha_{1..4}=0.2$). The filter, with every parameter from \code{laser2d.yaml}, is seeded with a broad Gaussian prior ($\sigma=1$\,m, 0.4\,rad) at the true start and updated as in \Cref{alg:mcl} with $d_{\min}=a_{\min}=0.2$. Seeds: 101, 202, 303.
  \item \emph{BBS relocalization.} On an asymmetric $6\,\mathrm{m}\times6\,\mathrm{m}$ room (two interior wall stubs break the four-fold symmetry, the world of the BBS unit test), $N=100$ poses are drawn uniformly over free space with uniform yaw, and one 180-beam scan is ray-cast per pose. To span easy to hard cases, each beam is replaced with a uniform-random spurious range with a per-query probability drawn uniformly in $[0,0.8]$, modeling people, glass, and objects absent from the map. The real matcher runs with the \code{bbs\_*} defaults ($W=10$\,m, $\pm\pi$ at 0.0175\,rad $\approx1^\circ$, $D=6$, \code{bbs\_max\_beams}${}=120$, which for a 180-beam scan gives stride 1, so all 180 beams are scored), centered on the room, so its window covers the whole map. The committed CSV was produced by the fixed-window matcher design; re-running the driver against the current matcher (symmetric window, feasibility rule) reproduces every column except wall-clock latency (\Cref{app:repro}). The driver calls the matcher with the fixed $W=10$\,m window, not the node's map-sized mode; in a 6\,m room both reach every grid cell. Seed: 20240607.
  \item \emph{ESKF under aiding dropout.} The real \code{Eskf} runs over a 60\,s 3D trajectory, a horizontal figure-eight $x=8\sin\omega t$, $y=4\sin2\omega t$ with $\omega=2\pi/30$ and a $\pm1.5$\,m altitude oscillation. The analytic specific force and angular rate are corrupted with exactly the white-noise densities and constant biases the process model assumes (\code{fusion3d.yaml} defaults), plus fixed true biases the filter must estimate from zero. The IMU runs at 200\,Hz, a simulated NDT source delivers 6-DoF poses at 10\,Hz and a simulated GNSS source positions at 1\,Hz, with noise matched to the filter's $\mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{meas}}$; both aiding streams are withheld for $t\in[25,35)$\,s. The PCL NDT registration stage that produces these poses in the node is not exercised here, and no experiment runs the three backends under one common Nav2 configuration. Seed: 20240517.
  \item \emph{BBS on large maps.} Square warehouse-like grids of side 20, 40, and 80\,m at 0.1\,m/cell carry border walls, \emph{repeated} shelf rows (0.5\,m deep at a 6\,m pitch with 1--2 random gaps each) and random 0.4\,m pillars; the repetition deliberately creates look-alike places. Kidnapped poses are drawn uniformly over free space and stratified into 60 inside and 60 outside the set that the fixed window searches ($-10$\,m to $+15.5$\,m about the map center; the 20\,m map lies entirely inside it). One 180-beam, 30\,m-range scan per pose receives the room study's clutter model, and the fixed-window matcher (a verbatim copy of the v0.1 matcher, with asymmetric root anchoring and no feasibility rule; \Cref{app:repro}), centered on the map midpoint with $W=10$\,m, and the current map-sized matcher process the identical scan. Seed: 20260928.
  \item \emph{Multi-scan verification.} On the maps, poses, and first scans of the large-map study (same seed and draw order, so the single-scan rows reproduce it exactly), the robot then drives a short random trajectory: 0.3\,m forward steps with $\mathcal{N}(0,0.15\,\mathrm{rad})$ heading changes, an in-place turn when a step would leave free space, and one scan per step with the query's clutter fraction. The verifier receives odometry increments corrupted by $\sigma=0.02|d|+0.005$\,m per axis and $0.01+0.02|\Delta\theta|$\,rad. Success is judged against the true pose at the scan where the method commits. Seed: 20260928 (study); 20261105 for the parameter-selection run of \Cref{sec:verify}.
  \item \emph{ESKF Monte Carlo NEES.} $N=100$ runs of the ESKF scenario above in which the initial position, velocity, and attitude errors and the true IMU biases are drawn from the filter's prior $\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{P}_0)$, and the true biases then random-walk with the filter's bias process noise, so that the prior, the sensor and bias noise, and the aiding noise all match the filter's model; the one unmodeled effect is that the IMU samples an analytic trajectory, whose integration between samples the zero-order-hold process model does not represent exactly. Every 0.5\,s we average the normalized estimation error squared $\epsilon=\mathbf{e}^{\T}\mathbf{P}^{-1}\mathbf{e}$ over the runs, for the full 15-D error and for each 3-D block, and compare it with the two-sided 95\% interval $[\chi^2_{0.025}(N n)/N,\ \chi^2_{0.975}(N n)/N]$ for $n$ degrees of freedom \citep{barshalom2001estimation}. The shipped filter and the reset-Jacobian variant process identical data. No parameter was tuned on these runs. Seeds: 20260929 plus the run index, and 20261029 plus the run index for the bias random walk.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Baselines, Metrics, and Hardware.} The synthetic experiments run no external baseline: every comparison in \Cref{sec:main,sec:ablation} is between configurations of \sname{} itself, because the synthetic worlds are designed to exercise \sname{}'s own cores. The public-dataset evaluation of \Cref{sec:public} compares against a 6-DoF scan-to-map NDT tracker and \code{nav2\_amcl}; Beluga \citep{puga2024beluga}, MH-AMCL \citep{garcia2023mhamcl}, \code{hdl\_localization} \citep{koide2019portable}, \code{hdl\_global\_localization} \citep{koide2021hdlgl}, CBGL \citep{filotheou2024cbgl}, and the multiple-hypothesis verifier of \citet{gao2019global} are not run. For the synthetic experiments we report position error (Euclidean, m) and absolute yaw error against synthetic ground truth, BBS success rate (position error $<0.5$\,m and yaw error $<10^\circ$), acceptance and false-accept counts at the threshold $\rho$ of \Cref{eq:bbsaccept}, windowed RMSE, the $3\sigma$ envelope, and Monte Carlo NEES for the ESKF, and BBS wall-clock latency. All synthetic accuracy numbers are deterministic; latency is machine-dependent and was measured single-threaded on the machine that produced the committed CSV. The public-dataset measures are defined in \Cref{sec:public}.

\noindent\textbf{MCL Tracking and KLD Adaptation.} \Cref{fig:mcl} and \Cref{tab:mcl} show that after convergence ($t\ge10$\,s, across all three seeds) the \code{laser2d} filter holds a mean position error of 0.105\,m (max 0.29\,m) and a mean yaw error of 0.032\,rad, i.e.\ 1.8$^\circ$ (max 0.18\,rad), with little spread across seeds ($0.105\pm0.007$\,m over per-seed means). The particle count starts at the 2000-particle ceiling under the broad prior, falls to the configured 500-particle floor after the first post-convergence update, and holds there for the rest of the loop, a $4\times$ reduction in per-update likelihood evaluations relative to the ceiling. Because the floor, not the bound $N^{\mathrm{kld}}_k$ of \Cref{eq:kld}, sets the count after convergence, this run does not show the set being sized to the posterior; the $4\times$ is the ratio of the configured ceiling to the floor.

\input{figures/bbs}
\input{tables/bbs_eskf_results}

\noindent\textbf{Global Relocalization.} Of the 100 kidnapped-robot queries, 97 succeed (\Cref{tab:bbs_eskf}); over all queries the recovered pose has median position error 0.037\,m (95th percentile 0.072\,m) and median yaw error 0.48$^\circ$ (95th percentile 1.74$^\circ$), consistent with the 0.1\,m grid and $\approx1^\circ$ rotation lattice. \Cref{fig:bbs} shows that the score fraction of \Cref{eq:bbsaccept} is an informative confidence signal: the three failures score in $0.21$--$0.27$ with more than 3\,m and roughly $90^\circ$--$180^\circ$ error, all below $\rho=0.40$, so the default threshold yields \emph{zero false accepts} while accepting 78 matches, all correct, and conservatively rejecting 19 correct fixes. The median score fraction is 0.632 and successes span $0.22$--$0.97$, so three low-scoring successes overlap the failure band. Median full-map query latency is 37.65\,ms.

\input{figures/eskf}

\noindent\textbf{ESKF Fusion.} With full aiding the windowed position RMSE is 0.016\,m (\Cref{tab:bbs_eskf}). During the 10\,s blackout the filter dead-reckons on the IMU alone and the RMSE rises to 0.125\,m, with a peak error of 0.256\,m at $t=34.5$\,s at the end of the blackout; the first aiding cycle brings the error back to 0.015\,m by $t=35$\,s, and the post-dropout RMSE is 0.009\,m (\Cref{fig:eskf}). The filter's own $3\sigma$ bound inflates from roughly 0.03--0.04\,m under full aiding to 0.663\,m and stays above the true error at every logged sample, i.e.\ the reported position covariance is not overconfident on this single seeded run. This is a one-sided envelope check on one run, not a consistency test. The NEES study below averages 100 runs against the 95\% interval and finds the position covariance too small while aiding is absent, so the two results answer different questions (one run against a bound, 100 runs against an interval).

\input{tables/bbs_largemap}

\noindent\textbf{Relocalization on Large Maps.} \Cref{tab:bbs_large} moves the kidnapped-robot study to maps larger than the room. For poses outside the set the fixed window searches, that matcher succeeds on only 2 of 60 queries on the 40\,m map and 0 of 60 on the 80\,m map, and at $\rho=0.40$ it \emph{accepts} a wrong in-window pose on 39 and 51 of them; the map-sized matcher recovers 50 and 41 of the same 60 poses. Inside that set the two matchers perform alike, as they should. The map-sized window does not, however, make acceptance safe. At $\rho=0.40$ the map-sized matcher accepts a wrong pose on 6 of 120 queries on the 40\,m map and 15 of 120 on the 80\,m map (fixed window: 43 and 57). Of these 21 false accepts, 13 are $180^\circ$ flips and none is closer than 4.95\,m to the truth; they are look-alike aisles, not near misses. No threshold removes them cheaply: the highest-scoring false match on the two large maps has score fraction 0.71, and only 94 of the 198 successes score above it. The feasibility rule does act: the fixed-window matcher, which lacks the rule, returned a pose inside an obstacle or off the map on 8 of its 300 queries, the current matcher on none. Global coverage also costs time: the median query takes 29\,ms on the 20\,m map but 1526\,ms on the 80\,m map, with a 95th percentile of 6374\,ms under heavy clutter.

\input{tables/bbs_verify}

\noindent\textbf{Verification against Aliasing.} \Cref{tab:bbs_verify} replaces the single-scan acceptance with the verifier of \Cref{sec:verify} on the same queries. False accepts fall from 6 of 120 to 0 of 120 on the 40\,m map and from 15 to 1 of 120 on the 80\,m map; pooled over both maps the false-accept rate falls from 21 of 240 [5.8, 13.0]\,\% to 1 of 240 [0.1, 2.3]\,\% (95\% Wilson score intervals, \citealt{wilson1927probable}). Of the 21 single-scan false accepts, 9 become correct fixes, 8 end as ambiguous, and 3 as no candidate; 1 remains a false accept. That residual case is a $180^\circ$ flip on the 80\,m map, 34.7\,m from the truth, which held 0.969 of the evidence score after nine scans: verification makes a confident wrong pose rarer, it does not rule it out. The price is small in correct fixes and moderate in time. The verifier commits 242 correct poses against 241 for the single scan over all 300 queries; it loses 8 fixes the single scan had (2 ambiguous, 6 rejected because the mean score fraction over the nine scans falls below $\rho$, these six all at clutter fractions of at least 0.66) and gains 9. Its median decision takes 1622\,ms on the 80\,m map against 1247\,ms for the single scan in the same run (three concurrent workers, so not comparable with the single-threaded latencies above), dominated by the wider top-8 search, and it needs the robot to move for eight more scans; a stationary robot in a look-alike place stays ambiguous by design.

\input{figures/nees}

\noindent\textbf{ESKF Consistency.} The NEES test gives a negative verdict (\Cref{tab:nees}, \Cref{fig:nees}): the filter is overconfident. Its time-averaged full-state NEES is 18.05 against 15 degrees of freedom, and at single epochs the run average lies inside the 95\% interval at only 31 of 120 epochs and above it (overconfident) at the other 89; it is never below it. The blocks locate the problem. Attitude is the worst block (above at 47 epochs, peak 5.91 against an upper limit of 3.50); position (inside at 104 of 120 epochs) and velocity are overconfident mainly during and just after the aiding dropout; both bias blocks are consistent (inside at 116 and 120 of 120 epochs). The published orientation covariance is therefore too small, and the position covariance is too small while aiding is absent.

\subsection{Sensitivity Analysis}\label{sec:ablation}

The committed runs also show the sensitivity to four mechanisms. All numbers below are recomputed from the same CSVs; no additional experiment was run for this section.

\input{tables/bbs_threshold}

\noindent\textbf{Acceptance Threshold.} \Cref{tab:threshold} sweeps $\rho$ over the recorded score fractions. Without the gate ($\rho\le0.20$) all three failures would re-seed the filter at a wrong pose; any $\rho\in[0.30,0.60]$ removes every false accept, and the shipped $\rho=0.40$ trades 19 rejected correct fixes for a margin of $0.13$ above the highest-scoring failure. Because a rejected match merely waits for the next scan, whereas a false accept silently corrupts the posterior, we keep the conservative default; $\rho=0.30$ would have accepted 16 more correct fixes on this data.

\input{tables/bbs_clutter}

\noindent\textbf{Clutter.} \Cref{tab:clutter} shows how clutter affects the matcher. Below a clutter probability of $0.533$ every query succeeds and is accepted; in the top third the pose is still recovered in 30 of 33 cases with an unchanged median error, but the median score fraction falls to 0.353, so only 11 are accepted. Clutter therefore degrades the matcher's \emph{confidence} much faster than its \emph{accuracy}, and the gate absorbs the difference; all three failures occur in this top third.

\noindent\textbf{KLD Adaptation and Aiding.} Replacing KLD adaptation with a fixed set at the 2000-particle ceiling would cost four times the steady-state likelihood evaluations of the 500-particle floor (an arithmetic estimate, not a fixed-size run); averaged over each whole run, including the initial transient, the adaptive set holds 526.9 particles, 3.8$\times$ fewer than the ceiling, with the accuracy of \Cref{tab:mcl}. The effect of withholding aiding is reported in \Cref{tab:bbs_eskf}.

\noindent\textbf{Reset Jacobian.} Enabling \code{reset\_jacobian} on identical data changes the run-averaged NEES of \Cref{tab:nees} by at most 0.026 at any epoch and in any block, and leaves every in/above/below count unchanged, so the omitted reset Jacobian is not the source of the inconsistency; with 10\,Hz pose updates the injected attitude corrections are small, which is consistent with $\mathbf{G}\approx\mathbf{I}$. We have not isolated the cause; candidates are the zero-order-hold discretization of the synthetic IMU, which the process model does not account for, and the first-order treatment of the attitude--bias coupling.

\subsection{Public-Dataset Evaluation}\label{sec:public}

This subsection runs the particle-filter backends on two public datasets and compares them with a 6-DoF scan-to-map NDT tracker and with \code{nav2\_amcl}. It tests how each method starts from a wrong initial pose, and for \code{laser2d} also how it relocalizes with no prior. It does not measure tracking accuracy in general: the numbers come from 12 runs per setting, with three seeds, and we read a difference of one or two runs as noise. \code{fusion3d} is not evaluated on public data (\Cref{sec:disc}).

\noindent\textbf{Code version.} The code evaluated is tag \code{paper-v2.1} of the repository (created on the posting commit). The experiments ran on commit \code{36a92ac} of the branch \code{exp/amcl-baseline}. The code contains two options that the synthetic experiments above do not use. The first is a tempered likelihood for \code{ndt3d}: \code{ndt\_effective\_points} sets how many independent points a scan counts for, and \code{ndt\_min\_sigma} puts a floor under the spread of every NDT cell. The second is \code{ground\_height\_from\_map}, which builds a ground height grid from the map at startup and places each particle's cloud at the ground height under that particle. Both are off by default. \code{laser2d} and the AMCL baseline use no such option.

\noindent\textbf{Datasets.} \emph{NCLT} \citep{carlevarisbianco2016nclt} (Open Database License 1.0; contents under the Database Contents License) provides a Velodyne HDL-32E, wheel odometry, and a ground truth from SLAM. The prior map is built from session 2012-01-08 (every fifth scan placed with the ground truth, 0.2\,m voxels). The query is session 2012-02-04, four weeks later, with four segments that start at 400, 1400, 2400, and 3400\,s. The route climbs and descends about 18\,m. \emph{MIT Stata Center} \citep{fallon2013stata} (CC BY 3.0 Unported) provides a 2D laser scanner and ground-truth poses for floor 2. The map is built from session 2012-04-06 by marking every 0.05\,m cell that holds at least five scan endpoints placed at the ground-truth poses, and the query is session 2012-04-02, four days earlier. A map built from ground-truth poses is optimistic, because a SLAM map would carry its own drift. The scan files carry no odometry, so we synthesize it from ground-truth increments (translation scale 1.03, rotation gain 1.03, 1\,\% Gaussian noise on each increment, constant yaw bias 0.02$^\circ$/s). The values are fixed and not tuned, and they are not the robot's wheel odometry.

\noindent\textbf{Protocol.} The initialization protocol was committed before any run, and the addendum for AMCL before the first AMCL run. The initial pose is the ground-truth pose at the first scan, moved by a planar offset of length $d$ in a random direction and turned by $\pm a$. Levels L0 to L6 use $(d,a)$ = (0, 0), (2\,m, $15^\circ$), (5\,m, $15^\circ$), (10\,m, $15^\circ$), (2\,m, $45^\circ$), (2\,m, $90^\circ$), (10\,m, $90^\circ$); level U gives no pose. The seeds 42, 1, and 2 draw the offset direction and the yaw sign, and each method receives the same offset. Each run lasts 180\,s; on NCLT it starts at the beginning of one of the four segments, on Stata at 200, 500, 800, or 1100\,s of the query session, so there are 12 runs per level. A particle filter is given the offset pose with a spread of $\max(0.5,d)$\,m and $\max(15,a)$ degrees and 2000 particles. A run is \emph{converged} if the planar error stays below 1\,m for 10\,s in a row, starting within the first 120\,s. The convergence time is the start of that window, and the RMSE after convergence runs from there to 180\,s. The RMSE therefore includes any later loss of the pose, whereas the convergence count does not show later losses.

\noindent\textbf{Methods.} \code{ndt3d} uses the ground grid with \code{ndt\_effective\_points}\,=\,5, \code{ndt\_min\_sigma}\,=\,0.10\,m, and motion noise $\alpha_{1..4}=0.05$, the best of six settings tried on the NCLT tracking runs (three are listed in \Cref{tab:elev}), which use the same sessions. The \emph{tracker} predicts a 6-DoF pose with the wheel-odometry increment and registers every scan to the map with PCL NDT \citep{rusu2011pcl} (resolution 1\,m, 30 iterations, 1\,m scan voxels, map cropped to 150\,m around the segment). It starts at the offset pose and has no use for a spread. We did not test other registration settings or a global registration front end for it. For \code{laser2d} and AMCL the NCLT data are projected to 2D: the map holds the cells with a map point 1.0--2.5\,m above the local ground, and the scan is built from points 0.8--2.3\,m above the sensor plane. We chose this height band after seeing the L0 results of seed 42 (16 band pairs were compared on these same segments), so L0 is not an independent test of it. A second band, fixed in advance and shifted up (map points 1.5--3.0\,m above the ground, scan points 0.85--2.35\,m above the sensor plane), was run on every level and is reported next to the first. AMCL is \code{nav2\_amcl} 1.1.20 (ROS~2 Humble), unmodified, with Nav2 defaults except the settings that match \code{laser2d.yaml} (500--2000 particles, update gate 0.2\,m and 0.2\,rad). It runs offline in lock step with simulated time, so it receives every scan once and in order. It reads the same map, scans, odometry, and offset pose as \code{laser2d}, and at level U it uses its own global localization (particles spread over all free cells; a variant with 2000--20000 particles is reported as U-20k). AMCL cannot be seeded, so its three seeds differ only in the offset and, on Stata, in the odometry noise.

\noindent\textbf{NCLT: Wrong Initial Poses, \code{ndt3d} against the Tracker.} \Cref{tab:nclt3d} shows that the particle filter recovers from initial headings that the tracker cannot. At an offset of 2\,m and $45^\circ$ (L4) \code{ndt3d} converges in 12 of 12 runs and the tracker in 4 of 12; at 2\,m and $90^\circ$ (L5), 12 of 12 against 0 of 12; at 10\,m and $90^\circ$ (L6), 11 of 12 against 0 of 12. At small offsets (L0 to L3) both converge in 11 or 12 of 12 runs. This is not a like-for-like comparison, and the result follows partly from it: the particle filter is told the initial spread, and the tracker, which takes one pose, cannot use one. The tracker is also more accurate once it has converged. The median RMSE after convergence is 0.148--0.169\,m for the tracker over L0 to L4, against 0.294--0.344\,m for \code{ndt3d} over L0 to L6.

\noindent\textbf{NCLT: Height Change and the Planar State.} With \code{ndt3d} defaults, which hold the cloud at a constant base height, 9 of 12 tracking runs show an excursion (\Cref{tab:elev}), and the largest error is 354.88\,m. The segments that start at 1400 and 3400\,s account for it: the maximum error exceeds 10\,m in 5 of their 6 runs, against 0 of 6 on the other two segments. The ground grid brings the largest error down to 8.35\,m, and the tempered likelihood settings to 1.50\,m. We read this as the motivation for the ground grid and as a confirmation of limitation (i) of \Cref{sec:disc}: the state is planar, and a model that holds the base height constant fails on a route with a large height change. The grid lets the model follow the ground; it does not add roll, pitch, or vertical state. The tracker estimates a 6-DoF pose and needs no such option.

\input{tables/nclt_2d}

\noindent\textbf{NCLT, Projected to 2D.} \Cref{tab:nclt2d} reports \code{laser2d} on the 2D projection. With band 1 it converges in 12 of 12 runs at L0, L1, L2, L4, and L5, in 9 at L3, in 6 at L6, and in 12 at level U (branch-and-bound relocalization); with band 2 the counts are the same except 8 at L3 and 9 at U. The RMSE after convergence is large at L6 (5.46\,m and 32.00\,m) because the pose is lost again later in the run, mostly on the segment at 3400\,s, where the descent is the likely reason the 2D projection fails (not confirmed). AMCL with Nav2 defaults fails on this projection even at zero offset: it converges in 6 of 12 runs at L0, and in 0 of 12 at level U, also with 2000--20000 particles. Checks on the driver did not change this: AMCL tracks on Stata with the same driver (below), the scans fit the map at the ground-truth pose, replacing the odometry by the ground truth on one segment still fails, and so does a run with 5000--20000 particles. The cause is probably the sensor model on sparse scans, since the height-band returns of the open campus carry little 2D structure, but we did not confirm it, and we did not test other AMCL parameter sets (for example \code{z\_hit}, \code{sigma\_hit}, or the number of beams), which could change these numbers. We therefore do not use AMCL as a baseline on NCLT, and we draw no conclusion about \code{laser2d} against AMCL from \Cref{tab:nclt2d}.

\noindent\textbf{MIT Stata Center: \code{laser2d} against AMCL.} On Stata, AMCL tracks and the two can be compared (\Cref{tab:stata}). \code{laser2d} converges faster: at levels L1 to L6 its median convergence time is 0.0--4.7\,s against 3.2--24.4\,s for AMCL, and its median RMSE after convergence is lower at every level (both are medians over the converged runs, and the RMSE includes later losses; the medians are over different sets of converged runs, and \code{laser2d} converges in fewer runs at L1 to L5) (for example 0.164\,m against 1.290\,m at L2). It does not converge more often. AMCL converges in 12 of 12 runs at L0, L1, L2, L4, and L5, in 9 of 12 at L3, and in 2 of 12 at L6, while \code{laser2d} converges in 12, 11, 11, 6, 11, 9 and 2 of 12 runs at L0--L6, so AMCL converges as often or more often at every level from L0 to L5, and the two are equal at L6. We therefore make no claim that \code{laser2d} recovers from offset starts more often than AMCL. For global relocalization (level U) branch-and-bound relocalization followed by tracking converges in 9 of 12 runs against 3 of 12 for AMCL's global localization (4 of 12 with 2000--20000 particles). The \code{laser2d} runs that fail at U are the three runs that start at 500\,s, where the verifier accepted a pose that the tracker could not hold. \Cref{tab:statareloc} gives the relocalization queries on their own: with the map of four days earlier the verifier is correct on 43 of 50 queries and accepts a wrong pose on 7, and with the map of three months earlier it is correct on 30, wrong on 8, and reports 12 as ambiguous. The verifier changes nothing on the first map, because its nine scans span 0.45\,s of driving and show almost the same scene. AMCL has no accept step, so these counts have no AMCL counterpart; its level-U runs are scored by the same convergence rule as every other level. In tracking runs seeded at the ground-truth pose, \code{laser2d}'s RMSE on the 119\,s query is 0.069--0.078\,m against 0.154--0.175\,m for AMCL. On the 1403\,s query \code{laser2d} loses the pose for about 500\,s in two of three seeds (RMSE 12.24\,m and 13.77\,m), whereas AMCL's losses are shorter (RMSE 1.24--2.03\,m).

\noindent\textbf{What These Results Support.} They support four statements. (1) On NCLT, \code{ndt3d} with the ground grid recovers from wrong initial headings in 11 or 12 of 12 runs where a 6-DoF tracker does not, when the particle filter is given the initial spread. (2) The tracker is the more accurate method once converged, and \code{ndt3d} with a constant base height fails on a route with 18\,m of height change. (3) On Stata, branch-and-bound relocalization finds the pose in more runs than AMCL's global localization, 9 of 12 against 3 of 12. (4) On Stata, \code{laser2d} converges faster than AMCL and with lower error afterwards. They do not support a claim that \code{laser2d} converges from offset starts more often than AMCL, a claim about AMCL on NCLT, or any claim about \code{fusion3d}.
