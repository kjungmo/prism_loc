\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\noindent\textbf{Monte Carlo Localization.}
Particle-filter localization was introduced by \citet{dellaert1999montecarlo} and \citet{fox1999efficient}, who represent the pose posterior as a weighted sample set updated by motion-model prediction, sensor-likelihood weighting, and resampling, and who demonstrated global localization from a uniform prior on real robots. \citet{thrun2001robust} later hardened MCL against accurate sensors and the kidnapped-robot problem via Mixture-MCL's dual sampler, and \citet{fox2003kld} made the sample size adaptive through KLD-sampling; the odometry motion model and likelihood-field sensor model we use are the textbook forms of \citet{thrun2005probabilistic}. The particle-filter core of \sname{} is a direct descendant of this line and of its \code{nav2\_amcl} lineage; we contribute no new filter theory. Sharing one such core across measurement models is not new either. Beluga \citep{puga2024beluga,ekumen2022belugarepo} is a ROS-agnostic, extensible C++17 MCL library; its ROS~2 nodes offer interface parity with \code{nav2\_amcl} and combine one particle-filter core with likelihood-field, beam, and 2D and 3D NDT sensor models, and its 3D NDT node estimates a full $SE(3)$ pose, which is more general than the planar state of our \code{ndt3d} backend. Earlier, \code{mcl\_3dl} \citep{watanabe2016mcl3dl} implemented 3D/6-DoF point-cloud MCL against a reference cloud for ROS, and \citet{martinrico2023nonplanar} replace Nav2's AMCL with an MCL over Gridmaps and Octomaps for non-planar terrain. Other work makes MCL recovery more reliable: \citet{akai2023reliable} estimates the reliability of the MCL estimate to detect failure and integrates a global localization method through importance sampling for quick re-localization, and MH-AMCL \citep{garcia2023mhamcl} maintains several particle populations, created by multi-scale map matching, and outputs the most reliable one. \sname{} instead delegates recovery to a deterministic branch-and-bound search (\Cref{sec:bbs}) followed by multi-scan verification (\Cref{sec:verify}).

\noindent\textbf{NDT Representations and Measurement Models.}
\citet{biber2003ndt} introduced the Normal Distributions Transform, modeling a scan as per-cell Gaussians and reducing registration to Newton optimization of a smooth score; \citet{magnusson2007scan} generalized the representation to 3D voxel grids for field deployment on mining vehicles. Both use NDT for deterministic scan-to-scan or scan-to-map registration that requires a reasonable initial guess. \citet{saarinen2013ndtmcl} brought the NDT into Monte Carlo localization (NDT-MCL), using it as the representation of both the map and the sensor data. The \code{ndt3d} backend of \sname{} follows this NDT-MCL idea in a simplified form: it scores raw subsampled points against the Gaussian-voxel map and reads the score out as a per-particle likelihood inside the shared MCL core, trading the quadratic convergence of Newton alignment for the multi-hypothesis robustness of the particle filter, so the 2D and 3D backends differ only in their measurement model.

\noindent\textbf{LiDAR-Inertial Fusion and Robot Software Systems.}
\citet{sola2017quaternion} is the mathematical basis of the \code{fusion3d} backend: the nominal/error-state decomposition, local angular-error parameterization, and predict--correct--inject--reset cycle are implemented essentially as derived there. On the systems side, \citet{moore2014generalized} established the configurable EKF/UKF fusion node for ROS, \citet{wan2018robust} demonstrated an industrial ESKF fusing GNSS-RTK, LiDAR, and IMU at centimeter accuracy as a production system tied to its own pre-built LiDAR maps, and Nav2 \citep{macenski2020marathon} treats localization as an external, swappable component. Tightly-coupled LiDAR-inertial odometry such as FAST-LIO \citep{xu2021fastlio}, and applied 3D map-based tracking systems \citep{koide2019portable}, are complementary: they produce ego-motion and maps, whereas \sname{} localizes within a prior map. Several systems already place more than one estimator behind a common interface. fuse \citep{locus2018fuse} is a ROS framework for sensor fusion by nonlinear least squares with plugin-based sensor and motion models; Autoware's pose-estimator arbiter \citep{autoware2024arbiter} launches NDT scan matching, YabLoc, Eagleye, and a landmark-based localizer, selected by a \code{pose\_source} argument, and provides a stop/resume mechanism driven by pluggable switching rules (the shipped rule enables all of them); and MOLA \citep{blancoclaraco2025mola} is a framework for LiDAR odometry, map manipulation, and localization whose pipelines are assembled from reusable blocks. Estimator cores kept free of the middleware are likewise established practice \citep{puga2024beluga,pedrosa2019lama}. \sname{} is one narrow instance of this pattern: it places exactly three estimator families behind one ROS~2 topic and TF contract, each implemented as a library that is unit-testable without a middleware runtime. It covers a small part of the direction set out by the modular 2D/3D localization framework whose design \citet{macenski2023survey} report as proposed, and it is not a general localization framework.

\noindent\textbf{Global (Kidnapped-Robot) Localization.}
\citet{olson2009correlative} formulated 2D scan matching as exhaustive correlative search over a rasterized log-likelihood table with coarse-to-fine multi-resolution pruning, and \citet{hess2016realtime} bounded that search with branch-and-bound over a precomputed grid pyramid to run loop closure in real time inside Cartographer. \sname{} borrows exactly this branch-and-bound-over-pyramid primitive and applies it to relocalization against a known static map, a setting that existing tools already serve. \code{hdl\_global\_localization} \citep{koide2021hdlgl} offers a 2D grid-map branch-and-bound engine, among others, as a ROS service, which \code{hdl\_localization} \citep{koide2019portable} calls to reset the pose of its UKF-based tracker, whose corrections come from NDT scan matching; the global-localization tooling of \code{lidar\_localization\_ros2} \citep{sasaki2026globalloc}, documented in 2026, runs map-wide branch-and-bound over an occupancy grid, returns ranked candidates, and re-seeds an NDT or GICP tracker through \code{/initialpose} behind safety guards that include consecutive confirmation samples and a cross-check against along-corridor aliases. 3D-BBS \citep{aoki2024bbs3d} extends branch-and-bound global localization to 3D point-cloud maps. \citet{adurthi2023scan} uses a hierarchical, max-pooled scan-to-map matcher to enrich the particles of a 3D-LiDAR particle filter before resampling. Other single-scan 2D methods include CBGL \citep{filotheou2024cbgl} and the method of \citet{zhang2026krp}, which samples positional hypotheses over traversable space with a rapidly-exploring random tree, a restriction similar in spirit to our rule that candidates lie in non-occupied cells. Feeding global-matching or global-localization results into a particle filter is itself established \citep{adurthi2023scan,garcia2023mhamcl,akai2023reliable}; what \sname{} adds is a specific wiring to its own MCL core: a 2D branch-and-bound search over the non-occupied cells of a map-sized window, verified over several scans (\Cref{sec:verify}) before it re-seeds the particle set once, in place of the heuristic recovery of classical MCL \citep{fox1999efficient,thrun2001robust}.

\noindent\textbf{Multi-Hypothesis Verification and Abstention.}
Deferring commitment until later observations disambiguate a place is also established. \citet{gao2019global} extract candidate poses from successive 2D laser scans and resolve them with an extended multiple-hypothesis tracker whose likelihood combines consecutive candidate poses with odometry; MH-AMCL \citep{garcia2023mhamcl} keeps several particle populations alive and selects the most reliable; \citet{adurthi2023scan} declares a vehicle localized only after the particle spread stays small over consecutive scans and the vehicle has moved; Reliable-loc \citep{zou2024reliableloc} monitors sequential pose uncertainty to switch localization modes in large street scenes; and \citet{akai2023reliable} estimates the reliability of an MCL estimate to detect failure. Concurrently with this work, \citet{zhang2026movefirst} proposed a selective layer for LiDAR-to-BIM global initialization that aggregates candidates from several submaps and issues typed COMMIT, DEFER, or AMBIGUOUS decisions, which symmetry-breaking motion can upgrade to COMMIT. The verifier of \Cref{sec:verify} is a specific and simpler instance of this idea for 2D occupancy grids: top-$K$ first-scan branch-and-bound modes, propagated by odometry and re-scored by a local branch-and-bound match, with an explicit ambiguous outcome before the MCL core is re-seeded. Its evidence score is a tuned heuristic, not a calibrated probability.
