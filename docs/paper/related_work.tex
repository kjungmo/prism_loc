\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Monte Carlo localization.}
Particle-filter localization was introduced by Dellaert et
al.~\cite{dellaert1999montecarlo} and Fox et
al.~\cite{fox1999efficient}, who represent the pose posterior as a
weighted sample set updated by motion-model prediction,
sensor-likelihood weighting, and resampling, and who demonstrated
global localization from a uniform prior on real robots. Thrun et
al.~\cite{thrun2001robust} later hardened MCL against accurate sensors
and the kidnapped-robot problem via Mixture-MCL's dual sampler. The
particle-filter core in \prismloc{} is a direct descendant of
this line of work and of its \texttt{nav2\_amcl} lineage; we contribute
no new filter theory. Sharing one such core across measurement models
is not new either. Beluga~\cite{puga2024beluga,ekumen2022belugarepo} is
a ROS-agnostic, extensible C++17 MCL library; its ROS~2 nodes offer
interface parity with \texttt{nav2\_amcl} and combine one
particle-filter core with likelihood-field, beam, and 2D and 3D NDT
sensor models, and its 3D NDT node estimates a full $SE(3)$ pose, which
is more general than the planar state of our \texttt{ndt3d} backend.
Earlier, \texttt{mcl\_3dl}~\cite{watanabe2016mcl3dl} implemented
3D/6-DoF point-cloud MCL against a reference cloud for ROS, and
Mart\'in Rico et al.~\cite{martinrico2023nonplanar} replace Nav2's AMCL
with an MCL over Gridmaps and Octomaps for non-planar terrain. Other
work makes MCL recovery more reliable: Akai~\cite{akai2023reliable}
estimates the reliability of the MCL estimate to detect failure and
integrates a global localization method through importance sampling for
quick re-localization, and MH-AMCL~\cite{garcia2023mhamcl} maintains
several particle populations, created by multi-scale map matching, and
outputs the most reliable one. \prismloc{} instead delegates recovery
from localization failure to a deterministic branch-and-bound search
(Section~\ref{sec:global}).

\textbf{NDT representations and NDT-based measurement models.}
Biber and Stra{\ss}er~\cite{biber2003ndt} introduced the Normal
Distributions Transform, modeling a scan as per-cell Gaussians and
reducing registration to Newton optimization of a smooth score;
Magnusson et al.~\cite{magnusson2007scan} generalized the
representation to 3D voxel grids for field deployment on mining
vehicles. Both use NDT for deterministic scan-to-scan or scan-to-map
registration that requires a reasonable initial guess. Saarinen et
al.~\cite{saarinen2013ndtmcl} brought the NDT into Monte Carlo
localization (NDT-MCL), using it as the representation of both the map
and the sensor data. \prismloc's \texttt{ndt3d} backend follows this
NDT-MCL idea in a simplified form: it scores raw subsampled points
against the Gaussian-voxel map and reads the score out as a
per-particle likelihood inside the shared MCL core, which trades the
quadratic convergence of Newton-based alignment for the
multi-hypothesis robustness of the particle filter, and lets the 2D and
3D backends differ only in their measurement model.

\textbf{LiDAR-inertial and GNSS fusion.}
Sol\`a's tutorial~\cite{sola2017quaternion} is the mathematical basis
of \prismloc's fusion backend: the nominal/error-state
decomposition, local angular-error parameterization, and
predict--correct--inject--reset cycle are implemented essentially as
derived there. On the systems side, Moore and
Stouch's \texttt{robot\_localization}~\cite{moore2014generalized}
established the pattern of a generalized, configurable EKF/UKF fusion
node for ROS, and Wan et al.~\cite{wan2018robust} demonstrated an
industrial ESKF fusing GNSS-RTK, LiDAR, and IMU at centimeter accuracy
as a production system tied to its own pre-built LiDAR maps.
Tightly-coupled LiDAR-inertial
odometry such as FAST-LIO~\cite{xu2021fastlio}, and applied 3D
map-based tracking systems such as Koide et
al.~\cite{koide2019portable}, are complementary: they produce
ego-motion and maps, whereas \prismloc{} localizes within a
prior map. Relative to these, our \texttt{fusion3d} backend claims no
estimator novelty; it packages a textbook ESKF as a ROS-free core
behind the same ROS~2 contract as the two MCL backends, so that
odometry-only, LiDAR-only, and GNSS-aided configurations are
deployment choices rather than stacks with different interfaces.

\textbf{Global (kidnapped-robot) localization.}
Olson~\cite{olson2009correlative} formulated 2D scan matching as
exhaustive correlative search over a rasterized log-likelihood table
with coarse-to-fine multi-resolution pruning, and Hess et
al.~\cite{hess2016realtime} bounded that search with branch-and-bound
over a precomputed grid pyramid to run loop closure in real time
inside Cartographer. \prismloc{} borrows exactly this
branch-and-bound-over-likelihood-pyramid primitive and applies it to
relocalization against a known static map, a setting that existing
tools already serve. \texttt{hdl\_global\_localization}~\cite{koide2021hdlgl}
offers a 2D grid-map branch-and-bound engine, among others, as a ROS
service, which \texttt{hdl\_localization}~\cite{koide2019portable}
calls to reset the pose of its UKF-based tracker, whose corrections
come from NDT scan matching; the global-localization tooling of
\texttt{lidar\_localization\_ros2}~\cite{sasaki2026globalloc},
documented in 2026, runs map-wide branch-and-bound over an occupancy
grid, returns ranked candidates, and re-seeds an NDT or GICP tracker
through \texttt{/initialpose} behind safety guards that include
consecutive confirmation samples and a cross-check against
along-corridor aliases. 3D-BBS~\cite{aoki2024bbs3d} extends
branch-and-bound global localization to 3D point-cloud maps.
Adurthi~\cite{adurthi2023scan} uses a hierarchical, max-pooled
scan-to-map matcher to enrich the particles of a 3D-LiDAR particle
filter before resampling. Other single-scan 2D methods include
CBGL~\cite{filotheou2024cbgl} and the method of Zhang et
al.~\cite{zhang2026krp}, which samples positional hypotheses over
traversable space with a rapidly-exploring random tree. Feeding
global-matching or global-localization results into a particle filter
is itself established~\cite{adurthi2023scan,garcia2023mhamcl,akai2023reliable};
what \prismloc{} adds is a specific wiring to its own MCL core: a 2D
branch-and-bound search, with no submaps or pose graph to maintain,
whose accepted result re-seeds the particle set once, in place of the
heuristic recovery mechanisms of classical
MCL~\cite{fox1999efficient,thrun2001robust}.

\textbf{Multi-hypothesis verification and abstention.}
Deferring commitment until later observations disambiguate a place is
also established. Gao et al.~\cite{gao2019global} extract candidate
poses from successive 2D laser scans and resolve them with an extended
multiple-hypothesis tracker whose likelihood combines consecutive
candidate poses with odometry; MH-AMCL~\cite{garcia2023mhamcl} keeps
several particle populations alive and selects the most reliable;
Adurthi~\cite{adurthi2023scan} declares a vehicle localized only after
the particle spread stays small over consecutive scans and the vehicle
has moved; Reliable-loc~\cite{zou2024reliableloc} monitors sequential
pose uncertainty to switch localization modes in large street scenes;
and Akai~\cite{akai2023reliable} estimates the reliability of an MCL
estimate to detect failure. Concurrently with this work, Zhang et
al.~\cite{zhang2026movefirst} proposed a selective layer for
LiDAR-to-BIM global initialization that aggregates candidates from
several submaps and issues typed COMMIT, DEFER, or AMBIGUOUS decisions,
which symmetry-breaking motion can upgrade to COMMIT. The relocalization
decision described in Section~\ref{sec:global} is simpler than all of
these: the best match of a single scan is accepted or rejected by a
score threshold, and no hypotheses are tracked over later scans.

\textbf{Robot software systems.}
Nav2~\cite{macenski2020marathon} treats localization as an external,
swappable component (stock AMCL or SLAM Toolbox), and its marathon
deployment names low localization confidence as one of the two causes
behind most of its recovery triggers. The ROS~2 maintainers'
survey~\cite{macenski2023survey} catalogs AMCL's shortcomings and
reports a proposed design for a localization framework that builds 2D
and 3D localization systems atop modular components. Several systems
already place more than one estimator behind a common interface.
fuse~\cite{locus2018fuse} is a ROS framework for sensor fusion by
nonlinear least squares with plugin-based sensor and motion models;
Autoware's pose-estimator arbiter~\cite{autoware2024arbiter} launches
NDT scan matching, YabLoc, Eagleye, and a landmark-based localizer,
selected by a \texttt{pose\_source} argument, and provides a
stop/resume mechanism driven by pluggable switching rules (the shipped
rule enables all of them); and MOLA~\cite{blancoclaraco2025mola} is a
framework for LiDAR odometry, map manipulation, and localization whose
pipelines are assembled from reusable blocks. Estimator cores kept free
of the middleware are likewise established
practice~\cite{puga2024beluga,pedrosa2019lama}. As framed in
Section~\ref{sec:intro}, \prismloc{} is one narrow instance of this
pattern rather than a new algorithm: it places exactly three estimator
families (likelihood-field MCL, NDT-MCL, ESKF fusion) behind one ROS~2
topic and TF contract, with each estimator implemented as a ROS-free
library that is unit-testable without a middleware runtime. It covers
a small part of the direction set out by the modular 2D/3D localization
framework whose design the survey~\cite{macenski2023survey} reports as
proposed, and it is not a general localization framework. The accuracy
evaluation planned in
Section~\ref{sec:limitations} will follow the trajectory-error
conventions (ATE/RPE) standardized by the TUM RGB-D
benchmark~\cite{sturm2012benchmark}, applied to per-backend
\texttt{map}$\to$\texttt{odom} estimates rather than visual SLAM
output.
